What photosynthesis needs

Select a card to see the details.

Light

The energy source

Light

Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue-violet light and reflects green, which is why leaves look green.

More light means faster photosynthesis, up to the light saturation point, where the rate levels off.

Carbon dioxide

The carbon source

Carbon dioxide

It enters through stomata on the underside of the leaf. When stomata close, for example at midday in dry weather, photosynthesis slows sharply.

Water

The source of hydrogen and electrons

Water

Roots absorb it and xylem carries it to the leaves. All the oxygen released in photosynthesis comes from water, not from carbon dioxide.

The overall equation

6CO₂ + 6H₂O — light, chloroplasts → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Light reactions

On the thylakoid membranes

Light reactions

Water is split and oxygen is released. ATP and NADPH are produced to power the next stage.

Calvin cycle

In the stroma

Calvin cycle

ATP and NADPH are used to fix carbon dioxide and reduce it to sugar. It does not need light directly, but it depends on the products of the light reactions.

Quick check

Where does the oxygen released in photosynthesis come from?

The light reactions split water, and that is where the oxygen comes from. The carbon and oxygen of carbon dioxide end up in the sugar.
